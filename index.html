The real problem

Your bank shows you an EMI. It never shows you the interest.

Every month your payment splits in two. Early in a loan, most of it is interest — rent on money you still owe. Only a sliver chips at the principal. That ratio is the whole game, and no bank statement draws it for you.

How the prepayment works

One lump sum, and the whole curve dives.

A prepayment lands straight on the principal — the balance you owe. Interest is charged on that balance, so a smaller balance means less interest every remaining month. The saving compounds, and the loan reaches zero far sooner. That gap is months erased.

The choice every bank offers

Reduce the tenure, or reduce the EMI?

When you prepay, the bank asks one question: keep your EMI the same and finish earlier, or keep the end date and pay less each month? Same lump sum, very different totals.

Reduce tenure

usually saves more

Your EMI stays the same. You keep paying the larger amount against a smaller balance, so the principal clears faster and the loan ends sooner.
